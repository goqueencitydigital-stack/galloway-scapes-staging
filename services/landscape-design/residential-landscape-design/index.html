The complete guide

Residential landscape design in Waxhaw, NC: from site walk to finished install

The short answer

Residential landscape design shapes the front and back yard around your house and how you live in it. It starts with a site walk covering sun, soil, drainage, views and goals, then a bed layout and planting plan that frames the home, layered from groundcover to evergreen structure to focal trees, sized for mature growth and installed on properly prepared Carolina clay.

01Where does a good landscape design start?

With the house. Its architectural style, color, scale and the way it sits on the lot set the tone for everything around it. A brick traditional in Weddington, a modern farmhouse in Waxhaw and a craftsman in Marvin each call for different bed shapes, plant palettes and materials. The design should look like it belongs to the home, not like a template.

The second starting point is you: how you use the yard, how much maintenance you want, whether you have kids or dogs, where you want privacy, and which views from inside the house matter most.

  • The house: style, color, scale and entry
  • The site: sun, shade, slope, soil and drainage
  • The views: from the street and from inside
  • The use: play, entertaining, privacy and pets
  • The upkeep: how much care you want to give

02What makes front-yard design work?

Curb appeal comes from framing. Beds should be deep enough to hold layered plantings, generally deeper than builder-grade foundation strips, and their curves should be generous and simple. The entry should be the clear focal point, with the walk leading the eye to the door. Corners of the house are softened with taller evergreens or small trees so the home feels anchored to the ground.

The most common front-yard mistake is planting shrubs that will outgrow their space. A design sized for mature growth keeps windows clear and avoids constant hard pruning.

From Terry

Stand at the street and squint. If your eye goes straight to the front door, the design is working.

03How do you layer plantings?

Layering creates depth and year-round interest. At the back, near the house or along a fence, are the structural evergreens that provide a backdrop in every season. In front of those are flowering and textural shrubs. Then perennials and ornamental grasses, then low groundcovers and seasonal color near the edges and the entry. Small trees and specimen plants serve as focal points.

Repeating a limited palette of plants in groups reads as intentional and calm; one of everything reads as cluttered. Evergreens should make up a good share of the plan so the yard doesn't look bare in winter.

04How does the backyard fit in?

Backyards are about use. Design starts by locating the spaces you want: a place to sit, a fire pit area, a lawn for play, a path to the shed, a screen from the neighbors. Plantings and hardscape then connect those spaces. Privacy is often the top request in newer neighborhoods in Indian Trail, Fort Mill and Waxhaw, where lots are close together, and layered evergreen screens look far more natural than a single row of one shrub.

05What happens between design and installation?

You review the plan and plant list, and adjustments are made until it is right. Then installation starts with the ground: old plantings and weeds removed, beds cut and graded for drainage, compacted clay loosened and amended where plants will go. Plants are set at the correct depth, which is slightly high in clay so roots don't sit in water, then watered in and mulched. You get care instructions for the establishment period, and ongoing maintenance is available through the Lawn Program.

FAQ

Residential Landscape Design: questions, answered.

How much does landscape design cost in Waxhaw, NC?

Landscape design and installation cost depends on the size of the area, the number and size of plants, whether trees are included, how much old landscaping must be removed, grading and soil preparation needs, any hardscape such as gravel paths or edging, lighting, and access. A front-entry refresh with new beds and shrubs is a very different project from a complete front and backyard design with privacy screening and an outdoor living area. Galloway Scapes provides a free design estimate after a site walk, and the plan can be phased, for example front yard first, so you can invest over time while working toward a cohesive finished landscape.

What plants grow best in Waxhaw clay soil?

Many proven Southern landscape plants handle the red clay of Union County well when beds are prepared properly. Reliable choices often include hollies, distylium, loropetalum, abelia, Indian hawthorn, gardenias in protected spots, camellias and azaleas in part shade, crape myrtles and native trees such as redbud and serviceberry. Perennials and grasses such as coneflower, black-eyed Susan, salvia, daylilies and muhly grass also perform well in sun. Success depends less on finding a magic plant and more on matching the plant to sun, shade and moisture, and on loosening and amending clay so roots can spread. Galloway Scapes chooses plants for each spot on your property.

How long does it take to design and install a landscape?

Timing depends on the scope. A front-yard refresh can often be designed and installed within a few weeks of the first site walk, depending on the season and plant availability. Larger projects with backyard spaces, grading, drainage or hardscape take longer to plan and build. Installation itself for a typical residential project may take from a day or two to a week or more. Fall is an excellent time to plant trees and shrubs in North Carolina because roots continue to grow through cool months, though spring installations are very common as well. Galloway Scapes gives you a clear timeline with the written estimate.

Do you install the landscape design too?

Yes. Galloway Scapes designs and installs. The same team that walks your property and plans the landscape also prepares the beds, grades for drainage, plants the trees, shrubs and perennials, installs edging, gravel paths and lighting, and mulches the finished beds. Because grading, drainage and lawn work are handled in-house as well, there is no handoff between a designer and separate contractors, and one company is accountable for how the finished landscape looks and performs. After installation, ongoing care is available through weekly service or the year-round Galloway Lawn Program.

How do I make my landscape low maintenance?

Low-maintenance landscapes are designed, not just planted. Choose plants sized for their mature space so they don't need constant pruning. Favor durable evergreen shrubs and tough perennials suited to your sun and soil. Group plants with similar water needs. Keep bed shapes simple and edges defined so mowing and edging are quick. Use mulch at the right depth to suppress weeds and hold moisture, and limit high-maintenance annual color to a few high-impact spots near the entry. Fixing drainage problems before planting prevents many plant losses. Galloway Scapes can design specifically around how much time you want to spend on the yard.
