Local know-how

What Waxhaw yards need.

A lot of Waxhaw is newer construction — Millbridge, Cureton, Prescott and the neighborhoods off Providence Road and NC-16. Those yards tend to share the same problems: builder-grade sod laid over compacted red clay, thin topsoil, and lots that were graded quickly. That’s why our Waxhaw work leans heavily on soil prep, sod, aeration and drainage fixes, not just mowing.

Older parts of town, closer to Main Street and the rural roads toward the state line, bring mature hardwoods, big leaf drops in fall and beds that have grown in over the years. There it’s more about pruning, bed renovation, pine straw and keeping a large property sharp without a weekend of work.

Many Waxhaw neighborhoods have HOA standards for lawns and beds. We keep edges, bed lines and mowing heights consistent so you’re never the house getting a letter.

FAQ

Waxhaw: questions, answered.

Do you offer weekly lawn care in Waxhaw?

Yes. Waxhaw is our home route, so weekly and every-10-day mowing, edging and blowing is available on most streets, plus our year-round lawn program.

Why does my new-construction Waxhaw yard puddle?

Builders often grade fast and lay sod over compacted clay. Water can’t soak in, so it sits. Aeration helps mild cases; low spots and water running toward the house usually need regrading or a French drain.

Do you build patios and fire pit areas in Waxhaw?

Yes — gravel patios, fire pit areas, stepping-stone paths and garden spaces. See our hardscape design page for recent work.
