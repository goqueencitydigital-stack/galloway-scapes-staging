General

What areas does Galloway Scapes serve?

We’re based in Waxhaw, NC and work throughout Union County, NC and York and Lancaster Counties, SC — including Weddington, Marvin, Wesley Chapel, Indian Trail, Monroe, Indian Land, Fort Mill, Tega Cay and Rock Hill.

Are estimates really free?

Yes. We walk the property, talk through what you want and send a written estimate at no cost and with no obligation.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Galloway Scapes is licensed, bonded and insured, and holds a North Carolina Pesticide Application License and an Ornamental & Turf License.

Do you work with both Fescue and Bermuda lawns?

Yes. Our lawn program runs a different seasonal schedule for cool-season Fescue and warm-season Bermuda, because they need fertilizer, aeration and weed control at different times of year.

How do I know if I need a French drain or regrading?

If water sheets across the surface toward the house, regrading or a swale usually fixes it. If the ground stays soggy for days after rain, a subsurface drain like a French drain is often the better answer. We’ll tell you which after we see where the water goes.

Do you handle commercial and HOA properties?

Yes. Along with homes, we maintain commercial properties and HOA common areas, including entrance beds, seasonal color and cleanups.

How does the lawn program bill?

Flat monthly pricing, due the first week of each month, with service all year. You can cancel any time with written notice; service continues through the end of that month.

What happens after I submit the estimate form?

We review your note and photos (if you mention them) and call or text to schedule a walkthrough. If it’s urgent, like water heading toward the foundation, call (803) 487-5240.

Lawn Maintenance

Lawn Maintenance service →
How often do you mow?

Every 7–10 days during the growing season, adjusted for weather and growth.

What’s the difference between weekly lawn care and the lawn program?

Weekly lawn care is mowing, edging and blowing. The lawn program bundles that with weed control, quarterly pruning, seasonal treatments for your grass type and 8 weeks of fall leaf cleanup for one flat monthly price.

Do you treat weeds?

Yes. We hold a North Carolina Pesticide Application License and apply pre-emergent and spot treatments as part of our program.

Is there a contract?

Weekly service doesn’t lock you in. Our year-round lawn program has a flat monthly rate and can be cancelled with written notice.

Bermuda or Fescue?

Bermuda for full sun and high traffic; Tall Fescue for shade and a softer, year-round green. We recommend based on your sun and use.

How long before I can use the lawn?

Light use after roughly 2–3 weeks once roots grab; full use after it’s been mowed a few times.

When is the best time to aerate in North Carolina?

Fescue: September–October. Bermuda: June–July.

Seed or sod for a renovation?

Seed is budget-friendly for Fescue in fall. Sod is instant and works for Bermuda or anytime you need it done now.

Mulch or pine straw?

Pine straw is lighter, cheaper and classic in the Carolinas; mulch lasts longer and gives a more finished, formal look.

When should hedges be trimmed?

Light shaping through the growing season; harder cuts in late winter. Flowering shrubs right after they bloom.

Do you do one-time cleanups?

Yes. Many clients start with a cleanup, then move to weekly service or the lawn program.

Do you haul leaves away?

Yes. We collect and remove them rather than leaving piles at the street.

Do you maintain HOA entrance beds?

Yes — entrance beds, common areas and seasonal color rotations.

Drainage

Drainage service →
French drain or regrading — which do I need?

Surface water moving toward the house usually needs regrading or a swale. Ground that stays saturated needs a subsurface drain. Often the best answer combines both.

Where does the water go?

To a safe outlet: a lower part of the property, a pop-up emitter, or an existing storm structure where allowed. We plan the outlet first.

Will my lawn be torn up?

Trenches are backfilled and the surface is restored with seed or sod as part of the job.

Is yard drainage different from a French drain?

Yes. A French drain is one tool. Yard drainage is the full plan, which may include a French drain plus grading and downspout work.

How far should downspouts discharge?

At least 10 feet from the foundation, and onto ground that slopes away.

Can aeration fix standing water?

For mild compaction, it helps. Real low spots need grading or a drain.

Can you work on lake-facing slopes?

Yes. Shoreline work may need Duke Energy or HOA approval; we flag that before starting.

How long does a French drain last?

Installed with fabric and clean gravel at the right slope, decades.

Do I need a French drain or just regrading?

Saturated ground: drain. Surface water flowing toward the house: regrading. We’ll show you which on the walkthrough.

When is the best time to prune?

Late winter for most trees; dead or hazardous limbs anytime.

Do you top trees?

No. Topping weakens trees. We thin and reduce properly.

How deep do you grind?

Below grade so turf or plantings can go over it.

How quickly can you come out?

Are you licensed to apply treatments?

Yes, we hold a North Carolina Pesticide Application License.

Are you insured for removals?

Yes, Galloway Scapes is licensed, bonded and insured.

Pressure Washing

Pressure Washing service →
Will pressure washing damage my siding?

Not with soft washing. We use low pressure and cleaning solution on siding and roofs.

How often should I wash my house?

Every 1–2 years in our humid climate.

Is it safe for shingles?

Yes, soft washing uses no high pressure on the roof.

Can you wash before staining?

Yes, washing is the right first step before a new stain.

Will it remove oil stains?

We can lighten most oil stains; very old ones may not fully disappear.

Can you level without killing my lawn?

Is regrading enough, or do I need a drain?

Surface problems often only need grading; saturated soil needs a drain too.

Do you offer weekly lawn care in Waxhaw?

Yes. Waxhaw is our home route, so weekly and every-10-day mowing, edging and blowing is available on most streets, plus our year-round lawn program.

Why does my new-construction Waxhaw yard puddle?

Builders often grade fast and lay sod over compacted clay. Water can’t soak in, so it sits. Aeration helps mild cases; low spots and water running toward the house usually need regrading or a French drain.

Do you build patios and fire pit areas in Waxhaw?

Yes — gravel patios, fire pit areas, stepping-stone paths and garden spaces. See our hardscape design page for recent work.

How far is Weddington from your base?

About 15 minutes from Waxhaw, so Weddington is part of our regular weekly routes.

Can you maintain a multi-acre Weddington property?

Yes. Commercial mowers, a full crew and a year-round program built for larger properties.

Do you install landscape lighting in Marvin?

Yes — path lighting, uplighting on trees and façades, and lighting along walks and drives.

Can you redo just the front beds?

Absolutely. Front-bed renovations are one of our most common Marvin projects.

When should I aerate in Wesley Chapel?

For Fescue, September–October. For Bermuda, early summer (June–July).

Do you mow larger rural properties?

Yes, we maintain acreage as well as neighborhood lots.

Do you serve all of Indian Trail?

Yes, Indian Trail is about 25 minutes from our Waxhaw base and on our weekly routes.

Do you take commercial landscaping work in Monroe?

Yes, including recurring maintenance, cleanups and pressure washing.

Do you serve Sun City Carolina Lakes?

Yes, we work throughout Indian Land, including the Sun City area. Check with your HOA on any approval needed for landscape changes.

Is South Carolina outside your service area?

No. Indian Land, Fort Mill, Tega Cay and Rock Hill are all regular service areas.

How much does yard grading cost in Fort Mill?

It depends on the size of the area, access and how much soil needs to move. We give a written price after walking the yard.

Can you fix erosion on a lake-facing slope?

Yes. We assess where water enters and exits, then combine grading, plantings and stone to stabilize it. Work right at the shoreline may need Duke Energy/HOA approval, which we’ll flag up front.
